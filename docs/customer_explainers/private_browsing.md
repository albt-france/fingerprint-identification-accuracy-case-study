# Why accuracy looks lower since your campaign: private browsing

*Customer note · SIMULATED example (fictional customer and platform) · one page*

## What you noticed

Since your acquisition campaign in mid-October, more of your visitors are treated as new and your fraud team sees
accuracy getting worse.

## What changed: who visits, not how well we recognise them

Your campaign brought many visitors who use **private browsing** (also called incognito mode). Their share of your
traffic went from about 7 in 100 visits to about 20 in 100.

Private browsing deletes everything the browser stored at the end of the session. The service then has fewer clues
to recognise a returning visitor, so these visitors are more often treated as new. That is true for every customer,
before and after your campaign.

When we compare like with like, nothing got worse:
- your **normal** visitors are recognised exactly as well as before;
- your **private-browsing** visitors are recognised as well as before too;
- there are simply many more private-browsing visitors, so the overall average goes down.

## A note on how accuracy is measured

Accuracy is measured on visits where we can check the answer against a reliable clue. Private-browsing visits rarely
have one, so they are under-represented in that measurement. In plain words: the headline accuracy figure describes
your normal traffic well, but it is **optimistic** for private-browsing traffic. We now show you both, separately.

## What we recommend

1. Look at accuracy **by type of traffic** (normal vs private browsing) rather than one overall number.
2. For the campaign audience, rely more on your own account information (for example, ask returning customers to log
   in) when a decision matters.
3. If private browsing stays high, we can review your rules together so that genuine new customers are not treated
   as suspicious just because they browse privately.
